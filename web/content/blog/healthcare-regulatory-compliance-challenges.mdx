One of the challenges Jendo Innovations faces is finding the right regulatory pathway for AI-enabled medical devices in Sri Lanka, while preparing for global markets that demand clinical evidence, patents, and a clear commercial model.

## Building beyond borders

Jendo Innovations is also in discussion to establish business centres outside Sri Lanka, including Switzerland and Singapore, markets with favourable environments for deep-tech MedTech ventures.

## Clinical validation

Jendo’s system is not fully commercialised yet, but the device is already being used by hospitals and laboratory chains, and has been clinically tested on over 800 patients.

## Affordable screening model

Under the current business model, the device is positioned at US$2,250 with an annual subscription fee of US$10,000. Each test is charged at US$8, designed to keep screening accessible in comparison with traditional pathways.

## Patents that unlock investment

The company proceeded to protect the invention first in Sri Lanka (pending), then in the United States (granted: USPTO Patent 10,912,464 B2), and in Japan (pending), through the WIPO Patent Cooperation Treaty route.

Investors want confidence that a product is ahead of the competition. A patent is key to proving the value of an invention, and winning the trust needed to scale preventive healthcare from Sri Lanka to the world.
